What is the Norwegian 4x4 workout?

The Norwegian 4x4 is a high-intensity interval workout. You warm up, do four hard 4-minute intervals at 85 to 95 percent of your maximum heart rate, take 3 minutes of easy recovery between each one, then cool down. It is one of the most studied ways to build aerobic fitness and raise your VO2max.

It gets its name from the researchers in Norway who tested and popularised it. The structure is the same every time, which is part of why it is easy to repeat and easy to measure progress against.

The structure

A single Norwegian 4x4 session looks like this:

PhaseTimeEffort
Warm upAbout 10 minutesEasy, rising to moderate
Interval 14 minutesHard, 85 to 95% of max heart rate
Recovery3 minutesEasy, 60 to 70% of max heart rate
Intervals 2 to 44 minutes each, 3 minutes easy betweenSame as interval 1
Cool down5 to 10 minutesEasy

That is four hard intervals in total. Start to finish, a session takes about 35 to 40 minutes including the warm-up and cool-down.

The heart rate zones to aim for

The intervals are the part that matters most. During each 4-minute interval you want to reach roughly 85 to 95 percent of your maximum heart rate. You should be breathing hard and unable to hold a conversation by the last minute.

During the 3-minute recoveries you drop back to an easy effort, around 60 to 70 percent of your maximum heart rate, so your heart rate comes down before you start the next interval.

If you do not know your maximum heart rate, a common estimate is 208 minus 0.7 times your age. It is only an estimate, so treat the percentages as a guide and adjust to how you feel.

Why people do it

The Norwegian 4x4 targets VO2max, which is the amount of oxygen your body can use during hard exercise. VO2max is one of the strongest single markers of aerobic fitness. Spending several minutes at a time near your maximum, repeated across four intervals, is an efficient way to push it up. The same session also builds endurance and helps your heart rate recover faster after hard efforts.

How to do the Norwegian 4x4

  1. Warm up for about 10 minutes until your breathing rises and you feel loose.
  2. Go hard for 4 minutes at 85 to 95 percent of your maximum heart rate.
  3. Ease off for 3 minutes at a light effort so your heart rate comes down.
  4. Repeat until you have done four hard intervals in total.
  5. Cool down for 5 to 10 minutes at an easy effort.

What exercise can you use?

Any activity where you can raise your heart rate and hold it steady works. Running is the most common, but cycling, rowing, the ski erg, and uphill walking or hiking all work well. The four by four structure stays the same. Only the activity changes.

Common mistakes

  • Starting the first interval too hard and fading before the fourth. Pace it so all four are strong.
  • Not recovering enough between intervals, so you never reach a high effort again.
  • Doing it too often. Once or twice a week is plenty for most people.
  • Guessing your effort instead of watching your heart rate, so you spend the interval below the zone that makes it work.

Questions people ask

How hard should the intervals feel?

Hard. During each 4-minute interval you should be at about 85 to 95 percent of your maximum heart rate, breathing hard and unable to hold a conversation. It should feel tough by the last minute, but repeatable four times.

How often should I do the Norwegian 4x4?

Most people do it once or twice a week, with easier training on the other days. It is demanding, so it works best as a hard session inside a wider training week rather than something you do every day.

Is it the same as regular HIIT?

It is a form of high-intensity interval training, but with a fixed shape. The 4-minute intervals are longer than the short bursts in many HIIT workouts, which is what makes it effective for VO2max in particular.
